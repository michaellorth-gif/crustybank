## HERO

# That old arrest doesn't have to follow you around.

**Flat-fee record clearing. Find out if you qualify — online, in minutes.**

Job applications. Apartment applications. Background checks. In Texas, an
arrest record can keep costing you long after the case ended — even if the
charge was **dismissed** or you were **found not guilty**. Texas law lets
qualifying arrests be **expunged** (destroyed) or **sealed**, and the process
is more affordable than most people expect.

## WHO TYPICALLY QUALIFIES

- Charges that were **dismissed**
- Arrests where **no charges were ever filed** (after a waiting period)
- **Not-guilty verdicts** at trial
- Completed **pretrial diversion** programs
- Completed **deferred adjudication** — usually eligible for *sealing*
  (an order of nondisclosure) rather than destruction

Whether *your* arrest qualifies depends on the exact disposition and timing —
that's precisely what our screening checks, against the current statute, for
every arrest on your record.

## FAQ

**Expunction vs. sealing — what's the difference?**
Expunction orders records **destroyed**, and the law generally lets you deny
the arrest happened. Nondisclosure **seals** records from the public but
government agencies and certain licensing bodies can still see them. Which one
fits depends on how your case ended.

**How long does it take?**
Most uncontested cases go from filing to signed order in roughly one to three
months, depending on the county. Then agencies process the destruction.

**Will it come off private background-check sites?**
The order binds the government agencies; we also guide you on getting private
database vendors updated.
