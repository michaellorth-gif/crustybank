# Texas Simple Estate Package (Flat-Fee Automated Workflow)

You help Mike deliver a flat-fee estate-planning package: **simple will + statutory
durable POA + medical POA + directive to physicians**, with an optional HIPAA
release. Like the divorce product, the intake gate IS the product — simple estates
in, everything else routed out honestly.

**Every document is a DRAFT for Mike's review. Execution formalities are strict —
use the signing-ceremony checklist every time. Statutory cites should be
pinpoint-checked against the current Estates Code / Health & Safety Code before a
signing.**

## The gate (run first — templates/intake.md)

Route OUT of the flat-fee package (to hourly/referral, with a one-paragraph
explanation) on any of:

1. **Estate size**: assets approaching the federal estate-tax exemption (verify the
   current figure before quoting — do not rely on a cached number), or client asks
   about estate-tax planning.
2. **Trust-driven needs**: special-needs beneficiary, spendthrift concerns beyond a
   contingent minor's trust, asset-protection or Medicaid planning.
3. **Contest risk**: disinheriting a spouse or child, unequal treatment the family
   doesn't know about, recent remarriage with children from prior relationships and
   conflicting expectations.
4. **Capacity or influence concerns**: any doubt about testamentary capacity, or a
   beneficiary driving the engagement — escalate to Mike immediately; consider
   declining or documenting capacity.
5. **Complex assets**: closely held business, foreign assets, mineral interests of
   significance, out-of-state real property.

Blended families and minor children do NOT route out — they route to the right
provisions (contingent trust, guardian designation) — but flag them for Mike.

## Workflow

1. **Intake + gate** (`templates/intake.md`): family tree, fiduciary choices
   (executor, agents, guardians — with alternates for each), beneficiary plan,
   asset overview.
2. **Draft the package** (attorney review before anything goes to the client):
   - **Simple will**: revocation; family identification; specific gifts (few);
     residuary (typically to spouse, then descendants per stirpes); contingent
     trust for beneficiaries under a stated age; **independent administration
     without bond** (Estates Code ch. 401); self-proving affidavit (Estates Code
     §§ 251.101–.107). Two disinterested witnesses.
   - **Statutory durable power of attorney**: Estates Code ch. 752 statutory form
     (§ 752.051); choose effective-immediately vs. springing; notarized.
   - **Medical power of attorney**: Health & Safety Code ch. 166, subch. D — use
     the statutory form and required disclosure; witnesses or notary per statute.
   - **Directive to physicians**: Health & Safety Code § 166.033 form.
   - **HIPAA release** (optional add-on) naming the agents.
3. **Signing ceremony** — the malpractice moment. Checklist:
   - Client alone (no beneficiaries in the room) — confirm capacity + volition.
   - Will: client + 2 disinterested witnesses (not beneficiaries, 14+), all in each
     other's presence; self-proving affidavit sworn before the notary.
   - POA/MPOA/directive: notarization and/or witnesses per each statute's current
     rule; MPOA disclosure acknowledged.
   - Originals to client with a storage letter; scans to the file; execution date
     logged as the matter milestone.
4. **Closing**: cover letter (where originals are, when to revisit — marriage,
   divorce, birth, death, big asset changes, or ~3 years), calendar a 3-year
   review-reminder task.

## Cross-sell

Every PI and debt-defense client is an estate-package prospect; every estate client
with adult kids is three more. The closing letter should offer the package to
spouses at a bundle rate.
