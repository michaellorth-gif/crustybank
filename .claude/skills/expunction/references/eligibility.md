# Expunction Eligibility Tree — Tex. Code Crim. Proc. ch. 55A

> Verified against current law August 2026 via Midpage (ch. 55A effective 1/1/2025).
> Articles marked ✓ were verified verbatim; unmarked articles are structural
> placements Mike should pinpoint-check in the current statute before filing.

Screen each ARREST separately. For each arrest, walk the gates in order.

## Gate 0 — Disqualifier scan (any hit → flag for Mike; usually fatal)

- Final **conviction** on any charge arising from the arrest (unless later acquitted
  on appeal, pardoned, or actual-innocence relief).
- **Court-ordered community supervision** on a charge from the arrest — including
  ordinary deferred adjudication — except certain Class C deferred outcomes.
  (Completed deferred adjudication → pivot to NONDISCLOSURE, below.)
- A **felony charge arising out of the same transaction** as the arrest, still within
  limitations or resolved unfavorably.
- Absconding/bail-jumping after the arrest (statutory exceptions apply).

## Gate 1 — Entitlement routes (Subchapter B: person entitled; court must grant)

**Route A — Acquittal or pardon.** Tried and acquitted, or convicted then pardoned or
granted actual-innocence relief. Acquittal route is immediate; watch the
same-transaction carve-outs. (Appellate acquittal / prosecutor-recommended expunction
route: art. 55A.101 ✓ — a district court, justice court, or municipal court of record
may expunge on appellate acquittal or on the State's recommendation.)

**Route B — No charging instrument ever presented.** ✓ art. 55A.052: entitled if no
indictment/information was presented for a misdemeanor from the arrest (or any
same-transaction felony), AND:
- **180 days** from arrest — Class C, no same-transaction felony charge; or
- **1 year** from arrest — Class A or B misdemeanor, no same-transaction felony; or
- **3 years** from arrest — felony, or any same-transaction felony charge; or
- **any time** — the State certifies the records are not needed for any
  investigation or prosecution (art. 55A.052(a)(4) ✓).
Entitlement applies regardless of limitations (art. 55A.052(b) ✓).

**Route C — Charge dismissed or quashed.** ✓ art. 55A.053: entitled if the
indictment/information was dismissed or quashed AND the court finds it was because:
- (A) completed **veterans treatment court** program (once-only, affidavit required,
  art. 55A.053(b) ✓);
- (B) completed **mental health court** program (once-only, affidavit required,
  art. 55A.053(c) ✓);
- (C) completed a **pretrial intervention/diversion** program (Gov't Code § 76.011);
- (D) presentment was made because of **mistake, false information, or similar
  reason indicating absence of probable cause** at the time of dismissal; or
- (E) the charging instrument was **void**.
Entitlement applies regardless of limitations (art. 55A.053(d) ✓).

**Route D — Dismissal + limitations expired.** Charge no longer pending, no final
conviction and no court-ordered community supervision, and the limitations period for
the offense has expired. The classic "wait out limitations" route where Route C
findings can't be obtained — commonly the practical route for ordinary dismissals.
Pinpoint-check its current article in subchapter B before filing.

**Special routes** (screen separately; different articles and standards): acquitted
identity-theft victims / identity used by another; certain decriminalized or
DPS-error records; unlawful-carry (former § 46.02) arrests; certain alcohol-related
Class C convictions of minors (Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.12); failure-to-attend-
school records. Also discretionary/court-recommended routes in later subchapters.

## Gate 2 — Petition mechanics

- Verified petition with statutorily required identifying information (full name,
  sex, race, DOB, DL number, SSN, address at arrest) plus offense charged, arrest
  date, arresting agency, cause number and court if charged (see Subchapter D,
  petition requirements — old art. 55.02 § 2(b) content survives recodification).
- List every agency/entity with records (see petition template checklist).
- Retention carve-outs: the State can seek record retention in limited situations
  (art. 55A.302 ✓) — expect it where the client remains chargeable for a
  same-transaction offense.

## The nondisclosure pivot (Gov't Code ch. 411, subch. E-1)

When expunction fails because of completed **deferred adjudication** (or certain
completed convictions, e.g., some first-time misdemeanors and DWI provisions),
screen for an order of nondisclosure instead:
- Sealing, not destruction: government/licensing entities still see the record.
- Offense exclusions (family violence, kidnapping, murder, trafficking, sex offenses
  requiring registration, etc.) and clean-record conditions during any waiting
  period.
- Waiting periods vary by section (§§ 411.072–411.0765 family) — some immediate on
  discharge/dismissal, some 2 or 5 years.
Run this screen from the current statute each time — it changes nearly every
session; do not rely on a cached tree.
