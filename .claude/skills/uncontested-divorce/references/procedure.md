# Texas Agreed Divorce — Procedure Reference (Tier 1)

> Statutory cites verified against current law August 2026 (Midpage), marked ✓.
> County-practice items vary — maintain the county notes table at the bottom as
> cases are completed.

## Statutory anchors

- **Residency** ✓ Tex. Fam. Code § 6.301: at filing, petitioner OR respondent must
  have been (1) a Texas domiciliary for the preceding 6 months and (2) a resident of
  the county of filing for the preceding 90 days. (Military and out-of-state-spouse
  wrinkles exist — §§ 6.302–6.303 — escalate if either spouse is out of state.)
- **Grounds**: insupportability, Tex. Fam. Code § 6.001 — no-fault; always the Tier 1
  ground.
- **Waiting period** ✓ Tex. Fam. Code § 6.702(a): no divorce before the 60th day
  after filing; a decree rendered early is not subject to collateral attack.
  Exceptions ✓ § 6.702(c): respondent's final conviction/deferred adjudication for
  family violence against petitioner or household member, or petitioner holds an
  active protective order / magistrate's order for emergency protection based on
  family violence during the marriage.
- **Waiver of service**: Tex. Fam. Code § 6.4035 — signed AFTER suit is filed;
  acknowledges receipt of a copy of the filed petition; sworn or by unsworn
  declaration (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 132.001). A defective waiver is the
  most common uncontested-divorce reversal point — treat sequencing as a hard rule.
- **Property division**: "just and right" standard, Tex. Fam. Code § 7.001. In an
  agreed case the decree recites the parties' agreement; still confirm the division
  is not unconscionable on its face (grossly one-sided divisions invite later
  attack and bar-grievance risk).
- **Name change**: restore a former name in the decree on request (Fam. Code
  § 6.706 practice) — always ask; it's free value.
- **Remarriage**: neither party may marry a third party before the 31st day after
  the decree (Fam. Code § 6.801-adjacent practice; court may waive) — advise in the
  closing letter.

## Sequence with timing

| Day | Step |
|---|---|
| 0 | File Original Petition (eFileTexas); standing order attaches where applicable |
| 0–7 | Send respondent: file-stamped petition + waiver + "I am not your lawyer" letter |
| 7–21 | Receive signed, notarized/declared waiver; file it |
| 10–50 | Draft decree; circulate; collect both signatures |
| 60+ | Prove-up (in person, remote, or by declaration per county); decree signed |
| Same day | File BVS Form VS-165; order certified copies |
| +1 week | Closing letter: certified copy, name-change checklist, 31-day remarriage note |

## Prove-up testimony script (petitioner, agreed case, no kids)

Short leading questions covering: identity; residency/domicile periods (§ 6.301);
date of marriage and separation; insupportability recitation (§ 6.001); no children
of the marriage under 18 and wife not pregnant; the decree divides all community
property and debts and the division is agreed and believed just and right; waiver on
file; name change requested/not; request that the court grant the divorce and
approve the decree.

## Common failure points (build checks around these)

1. Waiver signed before the petition was filed, or not notarized/declared → void;
   re-execute.
2. Decree missing a signature or an asset class (bank accounts, vehicles by VIN,
   tax years, debts by account) → prove-up rejected or post-decree fights.
3. County requires its own decree form/language (some do for e-file acceptance).
4. Standing order violations during the case (moving money, insurance changes).
5. Respondent goes silent after intake → convert to service + default track
   (Tier 2; default has its own prove-up and record requirements).
6. Petitioner "remembers" a house, pension, or pregnancy late → re-run the gate;
   do not squeeze it into Tier 1.
