# Texas Debt-Suit Procedure Reference

> Statutory cites verified against current law August 2026 (Midpage). Rule cites are to
> the Texas Rules of Civil Procedure; re-verify rule text against the current TRCP
> before relying on it in a filed document.

## Which court

| Feature | Justice court (JP) | County / district court |
|---|---|---|
| Jurisdiction | ≤ $20,000 excl. interest (Tex. Gov't Code § 27.031(a)(1)) | County: overlapping bands (e.g. Gov't Code § 26.042); district: unlimited |
| Governing rules | TRCP 500–507 + **508 (debt claim cases)** | General TRCP |
| Answer deadline | End of the **14th day** after service (TRCP 502.5(d)) | **10:00 a.m. Monday next** after 20 days from service (TRCP 99(b)) |
| Discovery | Only with judge's approval — motion showing reasonable & necessary (TRCP 500.9) | As of right; TRCP 194 initial disclosures due 30 days after first answer |
| Rules of evidence | Relaxed; judge may admit what it considers reliable, but hearsay/foundation objections still matter in practice | Full Texas Rules of Evidence |
| Default in debt claim | Plaintiff must prove damages — sworn statement of account or supporting documents (TRCP 508.3) | Default on unliquidated amounts requires evidence |
| Appeal | De novo to county court; perfected by bond/deposit/statement of inability within 21 days | Ordinary appeal |

Most consumer collection suits are filed in JP court. TRCP 508 defines a **debt claim
case** as a suit to recover a consumer debt brought by an assignee of a claim, a debt
collector or collection agency, a financial institution, or a person primarily engaged
in the business of lending money at interest — i.e., essentially every plaintiff this
practice will see.

## Deadline computation rules

- Count calendar days; do not count the day of service.
- JP: if day 14 is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the answer is due the next
  business day (TRCP 500.5). Filing by e-mail or eFileTexas before midnight is
  timely in most JP courts, but confirm the local court's accepted filing methods.
- County/district: "Monday next after the expiration of twenty days" — count 20 days
  from service, then go to the following Monday, 10:00 a.m.
- **Always calendar an internal deadline 3 business days before the true deadline.**

## After the answer — JP court sequence

1. Court sets the case for trial or pretrial hearing (often 60–120 days out).
2. File discovery motion + proposed requests promptly (TRCP 500.9). Judges routinely
   grant a modest set in debt-claim cases.
3. Plaintiff's law firm is running a volume docket; a case with an answered defendant,
   pending discovery, and a lawyer on the other side is a candidate for nonsuit.
4. If tried: JP trials are short bench trials. See checklist below.

## Trial evidence checklist (the plaintiff's burden)

The plaintiff must prove: (1) it owns the debt, (2) the defendant owes it, and (3) the
amount. Attack points, in the order they usually fail:

- **Chain of title / standing** (debt buyers): a complete, admissible chain of
  assignments from original creditor to plaintiff, each transfer covering *this
  account*. Generic bills of sale referencing an unproduced electronic file do not
  identify the account; object to any gap.
- **Business records foundation**: account records are hearsay (TRE 802) unless they
  satisfy TRE 803(6), typically via a TRE 902(10) affidavit served 14 days before
  trial. A debt buyer's employee usually cannot sponsor the *original creditor's*
  records — watch for "records of another entity incorporated into ours" testimony and
  object to personal-knowledge gaps.
- **Amount**: itemization of principal vs. interest vs. fees; charge-off statements
  alone often cannot support post-charge-off interest calculations.
- **The contract**: cardmember agreement matching this account and time period, and
  proof it was delivered/accepted; also controls attorney-fee and interest claims.
- **Attorney's fees**: recoverable on contract claims (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code
  § 38.001) only with proof of presentment and reasonableness — often unproven.

## Post-judgment landscape (drives settlement — see defenses.md)

- **No wage garnishment** for ordinary consumer debt in Texas (Tex. Const. art. XVI,
  § 28; Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 63.004) — only child support, taxes, student
  loans, and similar.
- **Homestead** exempt (Tex. Const. art. XVI, §§ 50–51; Tex. Prop. Code ch. 41).
- **Personal property** exemptions, Tex. Prop. Code §§ 42.001–.002 (verify current
  dollar caps before advising a client).
- Bank accounts CAN be garnished (non-exempt funds), and judgments last 10 years,
  renewable, with post-judgment interest — so "judgment-proof" is a posture for
  negotiation, not a promise to the client.
